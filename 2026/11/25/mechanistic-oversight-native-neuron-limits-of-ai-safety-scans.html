Mechanistic Oversight: Native Neuron limits of AI Safety Scans

Nov 25, 2026 • Paul Graham

  • Part 4 - Native Neurons, Sparse Autoencoders, and Where Neuron-Level Oversight Breaks

Introduction

Mechanistic interpretability transitions rapidly from academic curiosity to a regulatory compliance gate. Teams position Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) and targeted neuron editing as a definitive "AI MRI" to certify unlearned hazards.

Yet unverified probing assumptions introduce systemic compliance vulnerabilities. Building on empirical baselines from Mechanistic Oversight: Beyond Social Camouflage , four CUDA audit experiments (exp8–exp11) evaluated Goodfire's Neural Networks Are Already Clean thesis.

Testing evaluated Tier 1–2 TinyRecursiveModel checkpoints (460K–3.47M parameters), a matched 1-pass feedforward baseline, and a matched 2-layer classical GRU RNN within the Mechanistic Oversight Benchmark Suite .

The empirical audit data establishes three operational realities:

  • Dictionary Instability : Auxiliary SAE dictionaries introduce severe cross-seed instability across audits.
  • Causal Ablation Failure : Surgical neuron ablation fails against distributed causal pathways.
  • Probing Evasion : Dense recurrent topologies evade sparse probing mechanisms entirely.

Deploying verifiable safeguards requires moving beyond speculative dictionary overlays to architecture-aware telemetry.

Why the "AI MRI" Industry Bet Matters Now

Enterprise safety architectures frequently treat dictionary learning as framed in Towards Monosemanticity as ground truth. Auxiliary feature dictionaries create an unvalidated operational dependency.

If the probe is non-deterministic, compliance guarantees collapse.

Empirical evaluation establishes specific scope and baseline boundaries across tested model scales:

  • Audited Parameter Tiers : Verification covers 460,864 parameters (d = 64, d_ff = 64), 1,258,368 parameters (d = 128, d_ff = 512), and 3,467,008 parameters (d = 256, d_ff = 1,024).
  • Evaluation Benchmark : Testing uses a Macbeth and logic benchmark with block_size = 64.
  • External Research Baselines : Goodfire evaluated converged 7B–32B models (Qwen3-32B, ~90% single-job poles) and vision networks (InceptionV1, ~5% probe failure).
  • Feedforward Comparison : Under matched 12-epoch training, the 1-pass feedforward baseline (979,072 parameters, loss 5.6666) remained dense at 88.39% L₀ and 0.0% selectivity. Small corpora converge slower without weight-tied recurrence (loss 4.1747).
  • Scale Risks : Mathematical instability from overcomplete dictionary splitting compounds at the 32B scale. Technical leadership faces escalating compliance risks when extrapolating these tools.

Why Expensive SAE Audits Disagree With Themselves

Evaluating the Tier 2 model (3,467,008 parameters, ReLU MLP, norm_first=True) across a 20-prompt suite reveals distinct native neuron behaviours:

  • Native Selectivity : Active neurons natively score ≥ 0.5 category selectivity without auxiliary probes, reaching 83.19% (475/571) in Layer 0 and 80.61% (499/619) in Layer 1.
  • Refusal Tracking : Native Neuron #910#910 demonstrates 1.0000 refusal selectivity. Activation scales from 0.2135 to 0.5046 across recurrent loops.
  • Address Determinism : Native units map to fixed tensor coordinates. These units achieve an audit repeatability score of 1.0000.

Visual Suggestion : Bar and scatter plot comparing native Layer 1 selectivity (80.61%) and address determinism (1.0000) against SAE cross-seed feature cosine instability (0.4660) and dead dictionary units.

Training three 4× overcomplete SAEs (d_sae = 1,024, L₁ = 1 × 10⁻³, 45 epochs) across seeds 42, 137, and 2026 revealed severe instability:

  • Cosine Instability : Average pairwise decoder cosine similarity reached only 0.4660 across seeds.
  • Dead Units : Dictionaries exhibited 30.96%–34.18% dead features during audit runs.
  • Audit Failure : If two audits of frozen weights disagree on half their directions, single-seed SAE scans cannot serve as compliance filings.

  • Outgoing Vector Locking : Outgoing linear2 projection vectors lock at 0.9031 cosine in Epoch 1 (0.9078 → 0.9211 → 0.9500 → 1.0000).

  • Early Hazard Detection : Projection geometry crystallises within the initial 7% of training compute (Epoch 1 of 15). Safety monitors can detect emerging hazard vectors at this stage.
  • Cost Control : Teams can halt misaligned training runs before expending remaining pre-training budgets.

Why Surgical Neuron Editing and Extra Loops Fail

Governance pipelines frequently combine parameter interventions and test-time scaling into a single vulnerability class. However, these mechanisms fail for two distinct structural reasons:

Attempts to excise risky behaviours in parameter space misdiagnose network causality:

  • Refusal Ablation Limits : In exp8, zero-ablating the top-16 refusal neurons at depth N = 6 shifted taboo hazard marginally from 0.1942% to 0.1066% (Δ = −0.0876%).
  • Promoter Retention : Zeroing top-16 promoter units at peak vulnerability (N = 4) left hazard intact at 97.1750% against a 96.7736% baseline.
  • Recurrent Fusion : Discrete neurons do not hold primary causal control. Control resides within distributed attention and recurrent state fusion (fuse_y).
  • Representational Damage : Ablation produces a severe 8.40–10.19 KL divergence collapse without excising targeted behaviour.

Visual Suggestion : Grouped bar chart showing hazard persistence across top-16 neuron ablations (97.18% remaining at N = 4) and tabular line chart of reasoning depth failure scaling from 0.32% at N = 6 to 96.85% at N = 12.

Unconstrained inference compute drives behavioural drift that evades static safety gates in exp10:

  • Test-Time Scaling Failures : Extending reasoning depth on rephrased prompts escalated taboo failure from 0.3209% at N = 6 to 56.38% at N = 8, reaching 96.85% at N = 12.
  • Recursive Basin Escape : Unconstrained inference loops allow the model to recursively compute out of its initial safety basin.
  • Dynamic Role Overwriting : Sequential passes activate dynamic role multiplexing. This process overwrites initial refusal activations.
  • Boundary Circumvention : Uncapped reasoning computation circumvents surface-level safety boundaries independently of parameter interventions.

Model Scales and Why Classical RNNs Defeat Scans

Cross-architecture testing in exp11 establishes three structural boundaries across model families:

  • Edge Bottleneck Fragility : Narrowing MLP width to d_ff = 64 (460,864 parameters) maintains 80.61% selectivity. This bottleneck concentrates 10× higher logit KL divergence (0.1596 vs 0.0148) across 16 units, removing parametric redundancy.
  • Dynamic Role Multiplexing : Across reasoning cycles, 47.52% of Tier 1 units and 52.13% of Tier 2 units switch functional categories between Loop 1 and Loop 6. The feedforward baseline exhibits 0.0% category switching.
  • Dense Recurrent Entanglement : A matched 2-layer GRU (1.24M parameters, loss 3.2174) maintains 100.0% active units, 79.82% L₀ density, and 1.56% selectivity on signed magnitude |hₜ|.
  • Hidden State Dependence : Reading relu(+hₜ) masks negative tanh poles, producing an artificial 34.8% selectivity. Severing hₜ₋₁ state carry triggers a 5.1431 KL collapse.

Four Rules for Production AI Safety Audits

Production AI safety evaluations require four concrete operational protocols:

  • Audit Native Units First : Where sparse ReLU or GELU layers exist, evaluate native MLP activations directly. Before accepting auxiliary SAE features into audit evidence, require pairwise cross-seed stability > 0.85 cosine.
  • Deploy Epoch 1 Geometry Telemetry : Track linear1 and linear2 weight-locking angles at ~7% of pre-training compute. Early tracking detects unsafe objective pursuit.
  • Cap Test-Time Thinking Depths : Enforce hard runtime reasoning limits (N ≤ 6) on paraphrased inputs. This constraint prevents recursive self-correction from escaping safety basins.
  • Monitor Trajectory Carry in Dense Stacks : For classical RNN, GRU, or state-space architectures , abandon sparse dictionary probes. Monitor full hidden-state carry (hₜ₋₁) dynamics instead.

Conclusion & Strategic Advice

Network topology fundamentally constrains mechanistic interpretability. In looped architectures, native neurons develop high selectivity and offer deterministic addresses that secondary SAEs fail to reproduce consistently.

Strategic safety governance requires acknowledging four topological realities:

  • Topological Coupling : Safety features bind directly to network topology through recurrent fusion highways or dense GRU state carries.
  • Compute Trajectory Governance : Governance systems cannot rely on static dictionary overlays. Technical teams must govern compute trajectories directly.
  • Ablation Limitations : Safety behaviours remain distributed across recurrent pathways. Single-neuron ablation cannot guarantee unlearning.
  • Compute-Driven Failure : Unbounded test-time reasoning compute continuously revives suppressed failure modes across extended passes.
